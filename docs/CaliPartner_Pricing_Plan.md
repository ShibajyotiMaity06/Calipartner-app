This replaces the earlier pricing completely. I researched what your stack actually costs (database, servers, AI, email, store fees, GST), what Indian and global users pay for similar apps, and ran a month-by-month model for five years.

> **Owner Update (26 October 2026):** The live pricing has been updated to **Plus: ₹249/mo ($4.99), ₹1,499/yr ($29.99)** with a 7-day trial; **Pro: ₹499/mo ($9.99), ₹2,499/yr ($49.99)** with no trial. See the full official breakdown in [PLANS_AND_FEATURES.md](PLANS_AND_FEATURES.md).

> **How to read the numbers.** Costs are researched (sources at the end). Conversion, retention and install volume are **assumptions** anchored on published benchmarks, shown as three scenarios. Treat the profit tables as a range, not a forecast. "Profit" means cash left after servers, AI, store fees, GST, support and fixed costs, **before** marketing, your own salary and income tax (about 25% for a company).

### 1.5 The five things that decide whether it makes money

1. **Costs to serve are small. Price by willingness to pay, not by cost.** A user costs about ₹0.7 to ₹2.4 a month in servers (less as you grow). A Pro subscriber adds about ₹5.37 of AI at typical use and ₹29.57 even if they hit both daily caps every day. **Store fees and GST take about 31.5% of every rupee in India**, which is far bigger than any server or AI cost.
2. **Paid ads do not pay back in India at these prices.** An Indian paying user is worth about ₹588 over two years, but costs about ₹2,917 to win through ads (at ₹35 an install and 1.2% buying). Growth must come from **invites, partner matching, collaborations, content and word of mouth**.
3. **International users carry the profit.** In the base case about 41% of cash comes from the 8% of installs that are outside India, because a dollar-market subscriber pays 6 to 7 times more than an Indian one. With **no** international users the five-year base result falls by about 68%. Make the app and store listing work in English for the US, UK, Gulf and Singapore from day one.
4. **Annual plans are better for you and for the user.** Annual subscribers keep paying about three times longer than monthly ones, you get cash up front, and in India failed monthly payments are common (nearly a third of Google Play cancellations are billing failures).
5. **Price barely moves profit unless buyers are very price-sensitive.** Your draft and my recommendation differ by under 1% in five-year profit. The **structure** (what is free, annual-first, international users, how you acquire users) matters far more than ₹10 either way.

### 1.6 Your latest questions, answered

**GPS tracking in Free, and "is there a catch?"** No hidden catch, and you are right to move it. In v1 I had GPS in Plus only because it is the one tracking feature with a possible running cost (map display and stored routes). That cost is tiny if you pick the map provider carefully (section 8.7), and every free fitness app lets you track a run. It is now in Free. The real catches are about **building** it, not pricing it: Google Play wants a declaration for location access, background tracking drains battery, and a shared route can reveal where someone lives (so routes are private by default and the first and last 200 metres are trimmed when shared). Details in 8.7.

**5 scans and 10 messages a day: too little or too much?** It is a good size. Five scans matches the five meal sections, and 10 messages is plenty for a coach used as a daily check-in. At Haiku 5.5 prices a scan costs about ₹0.04 and a message about ₹0.08, so a typical Pro user costs about ₹5.37 a month and **a user who hits both caps every day costs about ₹29.57 a month** (against ₹68 a month received on the annual plan and ₹136 on the monthly plan). Cost is not the constraint here; accuracy on Indian meals is. Details and extra safeguards in 8.4.

**Remove 50% off for everyone, collaborations only.** Fine, and it costs little. If joiners respond to price like most buyers (elasticity 1.0), five-year profit is about the same (+0.1%); it ranges from -2.2% (very price-sensitive joiners) to +3.4% (not price-sensitive). The real risk is a weaker invite loop, which the model cannot see, so measure invite-to-join and join-to-paid from the first invites (8.2). Collaboration codes are cheap acquisition: a 20% commission on a half-price first year is about ₹41 per redemption, against about ₹2,917 per paying user through ads.

**Launch costs, apart from the $99 and $25 fees.** Almost, but not quite zero: you need **Supabase Pro at about ₹2,400 a month** from the first real users, because the free plan pauses after a week of inactivity, has no backups and its 500 MB database and 5 GB data transfer cover only about 300 to 500 active users. Everything else (R2, RevenueCat, EAS, Brevo email, Firebase) stays free at launch and AI costs a few rupees (₹0.04 a scan). First-month cash out is about ₹15,055 ($158); after that about ₹3,234 a month. Table in 2.4.

**Haiku 5.5 only.** Done. One AI service in the app with the model name in one setting, so a price or quality change is a one-line edit. Prices are in 2.2.

### 2.2 AI costs (Claude Haiku 5.5 only)

**Price:** $0.10 per million input tokens and $0.50 per million output tokens for prompts up to 100,000 tokens; **above 100,000 tokens in one prompt it is $0.50 and $2.50 (five times more)**. Cached input costs $0.01 per million, batch jobs are half price, and US-only processing costs 1.1 times more. An image costs about (width ÷ 28 rounded up) × (height ÷ 28 rounded up) tokens, up to 1,568. So a photo resized to 896 × 672 is 768 tokens.

**My token assumptions:** a photo scan is about 1,500 tokens in (image, instructions, your food list) and 450 out; one coach message (system prompt, your recent data, short history) is about 5,000 in and 700 out. Maximum-size versions: 2,500 / 700 for a scan, 20,000 / 1,200 for a message.

## 6. Why these prices (the sweet spot)

- **₹99 Plus** sits under the ₹139 that Spotify settled on after ₹199 failed in India. It is the price of two cups of tea a month and is an easy yes after a 7-day trial.
- **₹599 a year** is half of twelve months and costs the user ₹50 a month. Annual subscribers stay about three times longer; you also receive the cash up front, which is safer than monthly payments that fail more often on Google Play and UPI.
- **₹199 and ₹1,199 Pro** are under half of HealthifyMe's AI plan (₹2,499 a year) while giving a coach and photo logging. The price gap between Plus and Pro (₹100 a month) is small enough that users who want AI will take it, and large enough to cover the AI cost many times over.
- **No joiner discount.** Joiners are people a friend already convinced; the 7-day trial is the incentive. Partner codes (50% off the first year) go to people who would not have found you otherwise.
- **$5.99 and $39.99, $11.99 and $79.99** are the entry end of the US mid-market band ($29.99 to $49.99 a year for single-focus apps, $59.99 to $89.99 for multi-feature and AI apps). Regional price lists in the stores will lower them in other countries; set Gulf and Singapore at the US level and lower-income countries at about half.
- **Do not go lower to "feel cheap".** At ₹99 you already keep only ₹68 of each payment, and the price test (5.8) shows that cutting prices by 20% earns less unless buyers are quite price-sensitive.

Apple's INR price grid is fixed, so use the closest allowed tier if ₹599 or ₹1,199 is not offered; Google Play lets you set any price.

## 8. How it works

### 8.1 Entitlements (per person, not per room)
- Each person has one status: **Free, Plus or Pro**, with a source (store purchase, trial, or partner promo), checked on the server for every room, Partner Finder, challenge, fasting, plan and AI action.
- A room is created by a Plus or Pro user. **Every member uses room features only while they hold Plus or Pro.**
- If a member's plan ends, that person sees "Your room is waiting for you" (a rejoin screen) and the other members see their column as "Paused". Nobody is shamed and the streak is saved for 30 days.

### 8.2 Joining a room, and collaboration codes
**Joiners.** Someone who joins through a room code or link and has no plan sees the normal Plus paywall: "Join Rahul's room: 7 days free, then ₹599 a year", annual preselected. No discount. If they already hold Plus or Pro they just join.
- Measure from the first 2,000 invites: invite opened, joined, trial started, paid after the trial, still active after 30 days. The model assumes 0.125 paying joiners per new Plus payer. If far fewer than that pay, consider a 14-day trial for joiners (cost: nothing) before reintroducing any discount.

**Collaboration codes (partners only).** For creators, gyms, colleges, companies and dietitians you choose to work with. They are never shown in the app or on the website for everyone.
- **Offer:** 50% off the first year of Plus (annual), then full price. You can vary it per partner (a free month, a free year for a gym's staff).
- **How to run them (check each store's current rules before launch):** Apple subscription offer codes, Google Play promo codes or developer-determined offers, and RevenueCat promotional entitlements for free grants. Give each partner their own code (for example RAHUL50) so every redemption is attributed. Set a per-code limit (for example 500 redemptions) and an end date. One redemption per person.
- **Pay partners** a commission from the store and RevenueCat redemption reports. About 20% of what you receive on the first payment is ₹41 per redemption (4.2). A partner redemption is worth about half a normal subscriber and costs about ₹41 against about ₹2,917 for a paying user won through ads in India.
- The 7-day trial usually does not combine with a store offer code (one introductory offer per subscription); confirm in the store console and show the person what they get on the redeem screen.

### 8.3 Partner Finder flow
1. Free users can turn on "Open to a partner" and accept requests (this keeps the pool healthy and is safer).
2. Plus and Pro users browse up to 10 suggestions and send requests.
3. When a request is accepted, both see "Start your room". Whoever creates the room starts the 7-day trial (or pays); the other person joins and starts their own 7-day trial at the normal price.
4. If they do not want to start a room yet, the match is kept in **Favourites**. They can see the card but cannot chat. **My suggestion:** allow **one free reply** (the note plus a single answer) so the match does not feel dead. Safety rules (report, block, no photo or @username before acceptance) stay as in the PRD.

### 8.4 AI budget and limits (Claude Haiku 5.5 only)
- **Caps per Pro user:** **5 photo scans and 10 coach messages a day**, reset at midnight in the person's time zone. Chat-to-log counts as a message. Typical cost ₹5.37 a month; both caps every day ₹29.57.
- **Count only successful scans.** A failed call or a photo with no food in it does not use up a scan, but add a separate rate limit (for example 20 attempts a day and 6 requests a minute) so nobody can loop it.
- **Keep every call small** so cost stays predictable: photos resized to about 900 pixels on the long side before upload; a coach message limited to 500 characters; the last 10 turns of history or about 4,000 tokens, whichever is smaller; a short summary of the person's data instead of raw logs; answers limited to about 500 tokens. Every call then stays well under 20,000 tokens, far below the 100,000-token line where Haiku's price jumps five times.
- **Use prompt caching** for the fixed instructions (cached input costs a tenth of normal input). I did not count the saving in the numbers above.
- **Cost ceiling:** a hard stop at **₹40 per user per month** (the message: "Your AI coach is resting until the 1st"), plus the global daily budget switch already in your PRD (AIC-8). In normal use nobody reaches it; it exists for bugs and abuse.
- **Free and trial:** free users get 3 photo scans once (about ₹0.11); the Pro trial uses the same caps (at most ₹6.90 a week).
- **Is 5 and 10 the right size?** Log the event `ai_limit_hit`. If more than about 10% of Pro users hit the cap in a week, raise messages to 15 a day: it adds at most about ₹12 a month for those users.
- **Accuracy plan:** build the "suggest, then confirm" scan (2.2), test about 200 Indian meal photos before launch, and keep the AI behind one service so the model name is a single setting.

### 8.5 Paywall behaviour
- Annual preselected, per-month price shown, savings in a small label. Monthly is one tap away.
- No countdown timers or fake discounts. "Restore purchases" always visible. A "Have a code?" link for partner codes.
- Triggers: tapping any room feature, Partner Finder, fasting, meal planner, routines, photos, challenges (Plus); tapping the AI coach or AI photo logging (Pro, shown as an upgrade from Plus).
- Trial: one 7-day trial per account and per device, for Plus or Pro.
- Billing problems: use Google Play's account hold and grace periods and the store's retry; tell the person early and clearly; prefer annual plans.

### 8.6 Store products (four per store, one subscription group)
`plus_monthly`, `plus_yearly`, `pro_monthly`, `pro_yearly`. Ranking: Pro above Plus, so upgrades are immediate. RevenueCat entitlements: `plus` and `pro` (Pro includes Plus). Partner codes are offers on `plus_yearly`, not separate products.

### 8.7 GPS tracking: how to build it so it stays cheap and safe
- **Maps:** avoid map services that bill per map load (Google Maps, Mapbox) for a free feature. Use an open-source map library (MapLibre) with free or low-cost map tiles; self-hosting map tiles on Cloudflare R2 (no data-transfer fee) is an option to evaluate. Check the provider's free quota against your user numbers.
- **Storage:** save a route as a compressed line, about 20 to 50 KB per run, in your database or R2. A user running three times a week adds about 10 MB a year.
- **Permissions and battery:** track in the foreground with a visible notification while a workout is running. Background location needs a declaration and review on Google Play and a clear reason on iOS; keep the data use to tracking the workout. Sample every few seconds and pause automatically when the person stops.
- **Privacy:** routes are private by default. When shared with a partner or room, trim the first and last 200 metres so a home address cannot be read off the map. This also matches the PRD rule that GPS is never used for matching.

3. **Tax:** GST registration and filing as an app seller, TDS or withholding on store payouts, and whether a company or sole proprietorship is better. Ask your CA before launch.
4. **Store price grids:** the exact rupee tiers Apple allows, and Google's regional pricing.
5. **Apple's 15% rate** needs you to enrol in the Small Business Program; it ends above $1 million of yearly proceeds.
6. **Partner codes:** how each store limits offer codes, whether they combine with the free trial, and what the redemption reports show.
7. **Joiner conversion** against the model's 0.125 paying joiners per new Plus payer (8.2).
8. **Costs I assumed:** monitoring and support tools ($50 to $400 a month), support staff, moderation, accountant, domain.
9. **Maps for GPS:** the provider you choose, its free quota, and the Google Play location declaration (8.7).
10. **Free-plan limits** in 2.4, especially Supabase Free, which I recommend not using for a live app.

I also saved the model as `pricing_model.py` so you can change any assumption and re-run it, or I can turn it into a spreadsheet for you.
